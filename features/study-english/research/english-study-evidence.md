# Evidence for a walk-and-talk English tutor brief

Purpose: design a study note that an AI tutor improvises from during a 20-40 min commute walk.
Priority: principles with meta-analytic or first-party (author) backing, mapped to what a spoken session can do.

## 1. Corrective feedback: how to correct

-   Lyster & Saito (2010), *Oral Feedback in Classroom SLA*, SSLA 32(2): meta-analysis of 15
    classroom studies (N = 827). Abstract: "The effects were larger for prompts than recasts and most
    apparent in measures that elicit free constructed responses."
    <https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/abs/oral-feedback-in-classroom-sla/4999EE1C8379B2BF026B148EAF373CA1>
-   Li (2010), *The Effectiveness of Corrective Feedback in SLA: A Meta-Analysis*, Language Learning 60(2):
    medium overall effect for corrective feedback, and the effect was maintained over time.
    <https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/j.1467-9922.2010.00561.x>

Implication for a speaking tutor:

-   Use **prompts** (repeat-and-raise-eyebrow, "which word?", metalinguistic cue) more than recasts
    (silently reformulating the sentence). Prompts make the learner retrieve the form.
-   Judge progress on **free constructed speech**, because that is where feedback has the largest measured
    effect. Drills and single-sentence checks are weaker evidence.

## 2. Course balance: what a session should contain

-   Nation (2007), *The Four Strands*, Innovation in Language Learning and Teaching: a well-designed
    course gives roughly equal time to (a) meaning-focused input, (b) meaning-focused output,
    (c) language-focused learning, (d) fluency development.
    <https://www.wgtn.ac.nz/lals/resources/paul-nations-resources/paul-nations-publications/publications/documents/2007-Four-strands.pdf>

Implication: a walk session is not a vocabulary quiz. It needs all four:

-   input: tutor speaks a short passage or story, then asks comprehension questions
-   output: tutor asks open questions and lets the learner talk
-   language-focused: the target words and one repeated error pattern
-   fluency: same talk again under time pressure

## 3. Vocabulary: spacing and repetition

-   Kim & Webb (2022), *The Effects of Spaced Practice on Second Language Learning: A Meta-Analysis*,
    Language Learning 72(2): spaced practice outperforms massed practice for L2 learning.
    <https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/lang.12479>
-   Nakata (2015), *Effects of expanding and equal spacing on L2 vocabulary learning*, SSLA: expanding
    spacing is not reliably better than equal spacing; the plausible intervals matter less than
    whether spacing happens at all.
    <https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/abs/effects-of-expanding-and-equal-spacing-on-second-language-vocabulary-learning/D1D796306985C52F9BE7A1200AC50DB9>

Implication: words must reappear on later days, and the exact interval does not need tuning. A simple
recency window (last N days) is enough. No scheduler required.

## 4. Fluency: the 4/3/2 technique

-   Nation (1989), *Improving speaking fluency*: give the same short talk three times, for 4, then 3,
    then 2 minutes, to a new listener each time, keeping the message the same.
    <https://www.wgtn.ac.nz/lals/resources/paul-nations-resources/paul-nations-publications/publications/documents/1989-Fluency.pdf>
-   Thai & Boers (2016), *Repeating a Monologue under Increasing Time Pressure*, TESOL Quarterly:
    fluency gains; accuracy is not reliably improved by repetition alone, so pair it with feedback.
    <https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1101159>

Implication: a walk is a natural place for 4/3/2 because the walk itself bounds the time. Recast it as
one tutor listener in three rounds, and deliver corrections between rounds, not during them.

## 5. What this rules out

-   Markdown study notes read on screen: the artifact never reaches the eye.
-   Whole-corpus error mining: unbounded input, and the largest measured effects are in spoken
    production, not written error lists.
-   Vocabulary lists without retrieval: recognition is not the target behaviour.

## Confidence

-   Verified from abstracts or the author's own paper: items 1, 2, 4.
-   Direction only, no effect size retrieved: item 3 (Kim & Webb). Treat the strength as "spaced beats
    massed", not as a number.
-   My mapping from research to a walk session is inference, not a published protocol.
